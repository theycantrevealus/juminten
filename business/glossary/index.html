    Business Glossary

    1. Purpose

    This document defines the business terminology used throughout the Loyalty System.

    All business requirements, workflows, technical specifications, and implementation decisions should use these terms consistently.

    When implementing a feature, the AI agent must refer to this glossary to understand the intended meaning of business terms.

    If a term is ambiguous or its definition conflicts with an existing business requirement, ask for clarification before making assumptions.

    2. General Loyalty Terms

    Loyalty Program

    A business program that rewards customers for qualifying activities, purchases, or other business-defined actions.

    Customer

    An individual or entity participating in a loyalty program.

    A customer may have a loyalty account containing points, membership information, and redemption history.

    Loyalty Account

    The account associated with a customer that stores or references their loyalty-related information, such as point balances and account status.

    Loyalty Point

    A unit of value awarded to a customer according to the rules of a loyalty program.

    Points may be earned, redeemed, adjusted, or expired according to business rules.

    Point Balance

    The amount of loyalty points currently available to a customer for eligible activities.

    The system must distinguish between the available balance and any other balance categories if the business introduces concepts such as reserved or pending points.

    Reward

    A benefit that a customer can receive through the loyalty program.

    Examples include vouchers, products, services, discounts, or other business-defined benefits.

    Redemption

    The business process through which a customer exchanges loyalty points for a reward or another eligible benefit.

    A redemption may involve several processing steps, including eligibility validation, point deduction, and reward fulfillment.

    Redemption Request

    A request to redeem a reward using a customer's loyalty account.

    A redemption request contains the information required to identify the customer, requested reward, point cost, and relevant business context.

    The exact request fields are defined in the applicable technical contract.

    3. Redemption Processing Terms

    Redemption ID

    A unique identifier assigned to a redemption operation.

    It is used to correlate processing steps, logs, messages, and related records across services.

    Redemption Status

    The current business state of a redemption operation.

    Possible statuses must be defined by the approved redemption workflow and transaction-state specification.

    Do not introduce new statuses without reviewing the relevant business requirements.

    Eligibility Check

    The process of determining whether a redemption request satisfies the applicable business rules.

    Examples of eligibility criteria may include:

    • Customer account status
    • Available point balance
    • Reward availability
    • Reward-specific restrictions
    • Redemption limits
    • Campaign validity

    The actual criteria depend on the applicable business requirements.

    Eligible Redemption

    A redemption request that has passed all required eligibility checks.

    Eligibility approval does not necessarily mean that the redemption has completed successfully.

    Point Deduction

    The operation that subtracts the required number of loyalty points from a customer's account.

    Point deduction must only proceed after the required eligibility checks have passed.

    The implementation must follow the approved consistency and idempotency requirements.

    Bonus Fulfillment

    The process of delivering or activating the benefit associated with a successful redemption.

    Depending on the reward type, fulfillment may involve an external system, an internal service, or another business process.

    Successful point deduction does not automatically guarantee successful bonus fulfillment.

    Fulfillment Failure

    A condition in which the system cannot complete the required reward fulfillment.

    The expected recovery behavior, including retries, compensation, or manual intervention, must be defined by the approved business rules.

    Compensation

    A corrective business operation performed to address a previously completed operation that cannot be finalized as intended.

    For example, a business-approved process might restore points after a fulfillment failure.

    Compensation must not be assumed to be automatic. Its eligibility, conditions, and execution rules require explicit business approval.

    4. Transaction Terms

    Business Transaction

    A business operation that represents a meaningful unit of work, such as a redemption.

    A business transaction may involve multiple services and asynchronous processing steps.

    Transaction Record

    A persistent record used to track a business operation and its relevant state, history, or outcome.

    The exact responsibilities and schema of transaction records are defined in the architecture and data specifications.

    Transaction Status

    The recorded state of a business transaction.

    Transaction status and redemption status must not be treated as interchangeable unless the approved domain model explicitly defines them as the same concept.

    Idempotency

    A property that ensures repeating the same logical operation does not produce unintended duplicate business effects.

    For example, processing the same redemption message more than once must not deduct the same points multiple times.

    Duplicate Request

    A request that represents the same logical business operation as a request already received or processed.

    The system must distinguish a legitimate retry from a new redemption request.

    Retry

    An attempt to process an operation again after a temporary or recoverable failure.

    Retries must follow the approved retry policy and must not bypass business validation or create duplicate effects.

    Reconciliation

    A process that compares records across relevant systems to identify and resolve inconsistencies.

    Examples include comparing redemption records, point deductions, and fulfillment outcomes.

    5. Service and Messaging Terms

    Redemption Gateway

    The entry point that receives redemption requests and initiates the redemption workflow.

    Its exact responsibilities, validation boundaries, and response behavior are defined in the architecture specification.

    Eligibility Service

    The service responsible for evaluating redemption eligibility according to the applicable business rules.

    Point Deduction Service

    The service responsible for executing point deduction according to the approved point-accounting rules.

    Bonus Fulfillment Service

    The service responsible for fulfilling the reward associated with an eligible redemption.

    Notification Service

    The service responsible for coordinating or sending customer notifications related to redemption events.

    Notifications communicate business outcomes but must not be treated as the authoritative source of transaction state.

    Reporting Service

    The service responsible for providing reporting data and business insights.

    Reporting responsibilities must be separated from operations that authoritatively modify redemption or point-accounting state.

    Message Broker

    Infrastructure that transports messages between producers and consumers.

    The current implementation uses Apache Kafka for asynchronous communication between processing components.

    Producer

    A component that publishes a message to a message broker.

    Consumer

    A component that receives and processes messages from a message broker.

    Topic

    A named stream of messages in Kafka.

    Examples in the current workflow include:

    • redeem
    • eligibility
    • deduct
    • bonus
    • notification

    These names describe the currently discussed workflow and should be verified against the actual implementation.

    Correlation ID

    An identifier used to associate related operations or messages belonging to the same broader business workflow.

    Message ID

    A unique identifier for an individual message.

    A message ID and a redemption ID serve different purposes: one identifies a message, while the other identifies a business operation.

    Dead-Letter Queue (DLQ)

    A designated destination for messages that cannot be processed under the configured retry and failure-handling policy.

    The exact retry limits, routing rules, and recovery procedures must be defined in the messaging specification.

    6. Business Rules and Constraints

    Business Rule

    A requirement that defines how the loyalty system must behave from a business perspective.

    Business rules are documented in:

    • specs/business/business-rules.md
    • Relevant workflow specifications under specs/business/flows/

    Processing Order

    The required order in which business operations must occur.

    For the redemption workflow, the minimum established constraints are:

    1. Eligibility must pass before point deduction begins.
    2. Point deduction must succeed before bonus fulfillment begins.

    Additional ordering requirements must be documented in the approved workflow specification.

    Successful Redemption

    A redemption that satisfies the approved definition of business success.

    The exact conditions for success, including the required fulfillment outcome, must be explicitly defined in the redemption workflow.

    Failed Redemption

    A redemption that cannot reach the approved successful outcome.

    Failure handling must follow the relevant workflow and compensation specifications.

    7. Terminology Rules for Development

    When implementing or reviewing the system:

    1. Use the terms in this glossary consistently.
    2. Prefer established domain terms over ambiguous names.
    3. Do not use "transaction", "redemption", and "request" as interchangeable terms.
    4. Do not assume eligibility approval means redemption success.
    5. Do not assume point deduction means bonus fulfillment succeeded.
    6. Do not assume a retry is safe unless idempotency is addressed.
    7. Do not assume a failed fulfillment automatically restores points.
    8. Do not introduce new business statuses or transitions without updating the relevant specifications.
    9. If the glossary conflicts with an approved workflow, flag the conflict instead of silently choosing one interpretation.
    10. When a new business term is introduced, update this glossary as part of the requirements change.

    8. Open Questions

    The following definitions require confirmation from the business owner or project architect.

    • What exactly constitutes a successful redemption?
    • Which redemption statuses are required?
    • Are redemption status and transaction status separate concepts?
    • What should happen if point deduction succeeds but bonus fulfillment permanently fails?
    • Can a customer cancel a redemption after point deduction?
    • Are points restored automatically under any failure conditions?
    • What is the authoritative source for a customer's point balance?
    • Which operations require synchronous responses to the caller?
    • What are the retry limits and manual recovery procedures?
    • Does each redemption request require a client-provided idempotency key, or will the system generate one?

    These questions must be resolved in the appropriate business or architecture specifications before implementing behavior that depends on their answers.
